# LeakWatch3D

A short animated explanation of a smart water leak detection system:
**measure -> collect -> send -> analyze -> alert.** The whole scene is built at runtime
from C# (no prefabs or assets to wire up).

## Open and run

1. Add the `LeakWatch3D` folder in Unity Hub (Unity 6000.0.80f1) and open it.
2. Open `Assets/Scenes/SampleScene` and press **Play**.
3. The story starts by itself and plays through six scenes, then loops.

## Controls

| Key | Action |
|-----|--------|
| Space | Next scene |
| B | Previous scene |
| A | Auto-play on / off |
| L | Start the leak |
| R | Repair the leak |
| F9 | Save screenshots of all six scenes |

F9 steps through the scenes, waits 2 seconds on each and saves PNGs to
`LeakWatch3D/Screenshots/` (scene 6 gets one extra shot after the alert appears).

## The six scenes

1. **Supply** - water flows from the pump through one underground main to UB, then TP2.
2. **Measure** - Station A (UB-IN) and Station B (TP2-IN). All four sensors (Pressure, Flow Rate,
   Vibration, Location) keep their working mechanism visible and animating. The camera glides through
   them in order, about 3 seconds each; the active one is enlarged and its value pops out. It ends
   with a wide shot of the station with all four working together.
3. **Collect** - the data path is wireless only. Each sensor has a small Wi-Fi module (green board,
   metal shield, antenna, LED) and a battery pack. Its LED blinks and the value is packed with the
   location tag into a small packet at the module.
4. **Send (Wi-Fi)** - three Wi-Fi arcs in the sensor's colour travel from the antenna on the chamber's
   plastic lid to the router on the building wall (its LEDs blink); the signal then goes router ->
   monitor, and that row updates at that instant with a flash. A Wi-Fi icon on each station card
   pulses on every received packet.
5. **Leak** - UB's lower basement wall is cut away: hairline crack, thin spray, growing puddle.
   Vibration ripples run along the pipe to both stations.
6. **Analyze** - Station A and Station B are compared side by side; the pressure and flow
   markers leave the green normal band, and the line map pins the leak on UB.

## Readings (simulated, updated once per second, small noise)

| | Station A (UB-IN) | Station B (TP2-IN) |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | 4.2 bar, 12.0 L/s, 0.1 mm/s | 3.9 bar, 11.5 L/s, 0.1 mm/s |
| Leak | 4.1 bar, 12.1 L/s, 0.6 mm/s | 3.4 bar, 10.3 L/s, 0.9 mm/s |

Readings are rounded to one decimal, so the number that pops out of a sensor is exactly the
number the monitor shows. B is always slightly lower than A (UB takes its share, plus pipe friction); that normal
difference is the baseline. Detection is **rule-based** (the A-to-B difference leaves the
baseline in pressure, flow and vibration together). No ML model and no accuracy numbers are shown.

## 2-minute speaking script

**Scene 1 - Supply (about 15 s).**
"This is a campus water line. One pump supplies two buildings through a single underground
main. Water reaches UB first, then continues on to TP2. We can't see inside a buried pipe, so
we put sensors on it."

**Scene 2 - Measure (about 45 s).**
"This is Station A, just before the water enters UB. All four sensors keep working while we
look at each in turn. Pressure: water pushes a thin diaphragm, it bends, and a strain gauge turns
that bend into a number in bar. Flow rate: water spins a small turbine, and a pickup flashes each
time a magnet passes, so we count turns and get litres per second. Vibration: a small mass on a
crystal shakes with the pipe wall, and the crystal turns that into a signal. The fourth, Location,
measures nothing in the water. It is the station's ID tag, UB-IN, so we always know where a
reading comes from. Here they all work together."

**Scene 3 - Collect (about 15 s).**
"Nothing is wired. Every sensor has its own small Wi-Fi module with a battery. When a reading is
taken the module's LED blinks and the value is packed together with the location tag into a
small packet."

**Scene 4 - Send (about 25 s).**
"The chamber has a plastic lid so the signal gets out above the soil. Wi-Fi arcs in each
sensor's colour travel from the lid antenna to the router on the building wall, whose LEDs blink.
From the router the signal goes to the monitor, and that row updates at that instant with a
flash. The numbers are exactly the ones that came out of the sensors, and the Wi-Fi icon on the
card pulses every time a packet arrives."

**Scene 5 - Leak (about 15 s).**
"Now a small leak starts inside UB's basement. We cut away the wall to show it: a hairline
crack, a thin spray, and a puddle slowly growing. The leak shakes the pipe, and the vibration
travels along the wall to both stations, reaching the nearer one first and stronger."

**Scene 6 - Analyze and alert (about 25 s).**
"Station B is normally a little lower than Station A, because UB uses some water and the pipe
has friction. That normal difference is our baseline. When the leak starts, the pressure and
flow differences leave the green band and turn red, and vibration rises at both stations.
Because the leak is between the two stations, the map highlights the section from A to B and
drops a pin on UB: 'Leak detected, inside UB'. All data here is simulated and the detection
is rule-based, so our trained model can replace it later."
